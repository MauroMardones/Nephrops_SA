\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

\emph{Nephrops} stock in FU30 Division 9.a is considered a Category 3 stock. The advice is currently based on the 2-over-3 rule using the abundance index from UWTV surveys. The reference points for this stock are undefined and ICES cannot assess the stock and exploitation status relative to MSY or precautionary approach (PA) reference points. Stochastic surplus production model in continuous time (SPiCT) has been recommended by ICES for a number of data limited stocks to produce MSY advice. This WD shows some SPiCT explorations carried out in order to evaluate the potential of this model for Nephrops FU 30 assessment and propose this stock as possible candidate for the next WKMSYSPiCT benchmark. Results obtained are promising.

\subsection{Introduction}\label{introduction}

This report extends the exploratory framework by incorporating new SPiCT scenarios that emerged from discussions held after the last meeting (WKMSYSPiCT) held in December 2025. Specifically, a new set of scenarios is introduced, all configured consistently with the previous analyses by applying the same another alternative prior configurations (\texttt{RUN5–RUN8}) to each scenario. These new explorations include five additional scenarios (\texttt{SC5–SC9}) that differ in the combination of fishery-dependent and fishery-independent information used to describe stock dynamics. \emph{Scenario 5 (SC5)} combines total landings with the ISUNEPCA UWTV abundance index and the normalized ARSA yield series, providing a configuration based exclusively on survey-derived indicators to assess consistency between independent data sources. \emph{Scenario 6 (SC6)} integrates total landings with the ISUNEPCA UWTV abundance index and a standardised commercial LPUE, allowing direct comparison between fishery-independent abundance signals and fishery-dependent information. \emph{Scenario 7 (SC7)} represents the most information-rich configuration, combining total landings with ISUNEPCA UWTV abundance, normalized ARSA yield, and standardised LPUE indices to explore model behaviour under maximum data availability. \emph{Scenario 8 (SC8)} integrates total landings with a normalized ISUNEPCA UWTV biomass index and standardised LPUE, providing an alternative representation of stock dynamics based on biomass rather than abundance. Finally, \emph{Scenario 9 (SC9)} combines total landings with ISUNEPCA UWTV abundance, ARSA yield, and standardised LPUE, while explicitly accounting for a structural break in the ARSA survey by restricting its use to the pre-2012 period and relying on complementary indices thereafter.

The document provides a structured synthesis of these new scenarios and presents their main results, highlighting their relative performance, strengths, and limitations in comparison with previously explored configurations, with the aim of informing future benchmark discussions and potential model selection for \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} in Functional Unit 30.

\subsection{Methods}\label{methods}

\subsubsection{Study Area}\label{study-area}

The Gulf of Cádiz (FU30, Division 9.a) is located in the southwestern part of the Iberian Peninsula, bordered to the north by the Portuguese coast and to the south by the Strait of Gibraltar (Figure \ref{fig:areaconcept}). The area is characterised by a wide continental shelf that extends from the coast to depths of approximately 200 m, followed by a steep slope descending to depths exceeding 1 000 m. The seabed is predominantly composed of sandy and muddy sediments, which provide suitable habitats for Nephrops norvegicus.

\subsubsection{SPiCT description}\label{spict-description}

Surplus productions models are widely used in fisheries stock assessment, particularly for data-limited stocks. These models describe the dynamics of the total biomass of a fish stock over time, considering the balance between growth (production) and removals (catches). The basic equation governing surplus production models is:
\[
\frac{dB(t)}{dt} = P(B(t)) - C(t)
\]
where:
- \(B(t)\) is the biomass at time \(t\),
- \(P(B(t))\) is the surplus production function, representing the net growth of the biomass,
- \(C(t)\) is the catch at time \(t\).

In a graphical inspection, a surplus production curve typically has a dome-shaped form, indicating that production increases with biomass up to a certain point (the maximum sustainable yield, MSY), after which it declines due to density-dependent effects. The shape of curve dependes on the specific functional form chosen for \(P(B)\), such as the Schaefer model, Pella-Tomlinson model, or others. (Figure \ref{fig:surplusplot}).

The SPiCT model extends traditional surplus production models by incorporating stochastic elements and continuous-time dynamics. This allows for a more realistic representation of the uncertainties inherent in fish stock assessments, such as environmental variability and observation errors. The key features of the SPiCT model include:
- \textbf{Stochasticity}: The model incorporates process and observation errors, allowing for the quantification of uncertainty in biomass estimates and reference points.
- \textbf{Continuous-time framework}: Unlike discrete-time models, SPiCT operates in continuous time, providing a more flexible and accurate representation of stock dynamics.
- \textbf{Multiple data sources}: SPiCT can integrate various types of data, including catch data, abundance indices from surveys, and fishery-dependent indices such as CPUE and fishing effort.
- \textbf{Bayesian inference}: The model employs Bayesian methods for parameter estimation, enabling the incorporation of prior knowledge and the derivation of posterior distributions for model parameters and stock status indicators.

\subsubsection{Data Sources}\label{data-sources}

Annual Nephrops \textbf{landings} in FU30 show strong interannual variability over the analysed period (Figure \ref{fig:landings}), with increasing catches during the late 1990s and early 2000s followed by a sharp decline associated with management measures. Since then, landings have remained at lower levels with notable recent fluctuations, reflecting both stock availability and regulatory constraints.

Fishery-independent information is provided by the \textbf{ISUNEPCA UWTV survey}, conducted annually since 2014 (except 2020), which delivers absolute abundance and biomass estimates following ICES standards (\citeproc{ref-ICES2017WKNEP}{ICES, 2017}, \citeproc{ref-ICES2022WGBIE}{2022}; \citeproc{ref-VilaBurgos2022}{Vila \& Burgos, 2022}; \citeproc{ref-Vila2016}{Vila, Burgos, \& Soriano, 2016}), and by the \textbf{ARSA bottom trawl surveys}, which provide biomass indices for Nephrops in deeper strata of FU30 (Figure \ref{fig:landindex}). In addition, a \textbf{standardised commercial CPUE series}, derived from Spanish and Portuguese fleets, offers a fishery-dependent index of relative abundance.

Together, these complementary fishery-independent and fishery-dependent indices (Figure (\citeproc{ref-ref}{\textbf{ref?}})(fig:indices)) capture contrasting but informative signals on stock dynamics and constitute the empirical basis for the SPiCT model analyses.

Pairwise relationships among the different abundance, productivity and fishery-dependent indices were explored using a Spearman rank correlation analysis. Spearman correlations were selected to account for potential non-linear relationships and non-normal distributions in the time series.

\subsubsection{Previous Neprhops FU30 Scenarios}\label{previous-neprhops-fu30-scenarios}

\quad

Several SPiCT model configurations were explored for Nephrops FU30, varying in the inclusion of abundance indices, catch data, and model parameterisations (priors). This exploratory framework is consistent with the approach adopted in previous ICES Nephrops working groups, where alternative data configurations and prior assumptions have been systematically tested to evaluate model robustness and suitability for assessment purposes. The full set of SPiCT model configurations explored in this study is summarised in Table \ref{tab:scenarios}, including the input data used in each scenario and the corresponding prior configurations applied consistently across scenarios. This structured exploration allows for a transparent comparison of model behaviour and performance, in line with recent methodological developments discussed within ICES Nephrops expert groups.

The \texttt{Scenario\ 0} included catch data from 1994 to 2023 and the ISUNEPCA UWTV survey index from 2015 to 2023, reflecting the core data sources currently used in the assessment framework for this stock. Additional scenarios were developed to assess the effect of incorporating supplementary fishery-independent and fishery-dependent information, such as the ARSA survey index (\texttt{Scenario\ 2}), as well as alternative indicators derived from effort and CPUE series. Further scenarios explored the sensitivity of model outcomes to different assumptions regarding process error, observation error, and prior distributions for key biological parameters, following configurations commonly examined in recent Nephrops assessments (\texttt{Scenarios\ 3\ and\ 4}).

Explorations were conducted using data available up to 2025. Tables below summarise the different scenarios and model configurations tested. Fishing effort and CPUE were not included in any scenario, as survey-based indices are recommended when available. The autumn ARSA survey (SpGFS-caut-WIBTS-Q4) was also excluded from the analyses, as part of the stock is not accessible to the gear during this survey due to the reproductive behaviour of Nephrops. In November, ovigerous females remain inside their burrows and are therefore unavailable to bottom trawl sampling. For this reason, the autumn ARSA survey index has never been included in the FU30 WGBIE assessments.

The ISUNEPCA UWTV survey index is considered the most reliable abundance index for Nephrops in FU30 and is currently used as the basis for assessment and advice (\citeproc{ref-ICES2023b}{ICES, 2023}). This survey is conducted in the year of the assessment; for example, the UWTV survey planned for June 2024 will be used to inform the advice for 2025, delivered in October 2024. If the 2024 ISUNEPCA index were to be included in a SPiCT model, catch data for 2024 would not yet be available. This issue should be explicitly addressed if SPiCT is adopted as the assessment model for this stock in a future benchmark.

\subsubsection{New Scenarios in Nephrops FU30}\label{new-scenarios-in-nephrops-fu30}

Based on previous experts recommendations based on knowledge about surveys and fishery of \emph{Nephrops norvegicus}, \textbf{five additional modelling scenarios (Scenarios 5--9)} were defined, differing in the combination and temporal coverage of fishery-dependent and fishery-independent data sources (Table \ref{tab:newscenario}). Across all scenarios, \textbf{total landings} are included as the catch time series, while scenarios differ in the configuration of biomass- and abundance-based indices.

\emph{Scenario 5} combines total landings with \textbf{normalized biomass indices from the ISUNEPCA UWTV survey and the ARSA survey}, representing a configuration based exclusively on fishery-independent information. \emph{Scenario 6} integrates total landings with the \textbf{normalized ISUNEPCA UWTV biomass index} and a \textbf{standardised commercial CPUE}, allowing comparison between survey-derived biomass signals and fishery-dependent abundance information. \emph{Scenario 7} represents the most information-rich configuration, combining total landings with \textbf{normalized ISUNEPCA UWTV biomass}, \textbf{normalized ARSA biomass}, and \textbf{standardised CPUE}, thereby incorporating both fishery-independent and fishery-dependent signals across overlapping time periods. \emph{Scenario 8} combines landings with the \textbf{normalized ISUNEPCA UWTV abundance index} and \textbf{standardised CPUE}, providing an alternative representation of stock dynamics based on abundance rather than biomass. Finally, \emph{Scenario 9} was designed to explicitly account for a major structural change in the ARSA survey. Following a change in vessel and operational characteristics in 2012, only the \textbf{pre-2012 ARSA biomass series} is retained in this scenario to avoid potential methodological discontinuities. Post-2012 stock dynamics are instead informed by the \textbf{ISUNEPCA UWTV survey}, which provides a consistent biomass index from 2015 onwards, together with the \textbf{standardised CPUE} reflecting recent commercial fleet performance.

This scenario therefore provides a coherent data configuration that respects survey continuity while preserving both long-term and contemporary information for stock assessment (Table \ref{tab:newscenario}). All scenarios include total landings as the catch time series and differ in the configuration of abundance indices (Figure \ref{fig:indices}).

In light of the new observations arising from the initial analyses, an additional set of alternative prior configurations (\textbf{RUN5--RUN8}) was implemented to further test the robustness of the SPiCT scenarios. In these additional runs, the \textbf{bkfrac configuration was not used}, as preliminary results indicated that it strongly constrained model outcomes and could unduly condition estimates of stock status. Instead, RUN5--RUN8 focused on exploring alternative assumptions regarding the \textbf{production curve shape parameter (\emph{n})} and the \textbf{intrinsic growth rate (\emph{r})}, using a revised and more flexible prior structure.

In addition, \textbf{RUN6--RUN8 incorporated alternative priors on key process and observation error components}, allowing explicit evaluation of uncertainty assumptions beyond stock productivity parameters. Specifically, a prior was specified on the \textbf{catch process uncertainty} (\texttt{logsdb}), centred on a moderate level of variability, while additional priors were introduced to \textbf{reduce uncertainty in catch observation error} (\texttt{logsdc}) and \textbf{fishing mortality error} (\texttt{logdf}). These priors were intended to stabilise estimation of process and observation variance components and to assess the sensitivity of model outcomes to alternative assumptions regarding noise structure in the data-generating processes.

We further evaluated a subset of configurations (\textbf{RUN7}) in which \textbf{priors were specified on the observation model through an associated coefficient of variation (CV) on the abundance index}, allowing explicit testing of model sensitivity to assumptions about observation uncertainty. Finally, we explored the use of the default \textbf{SPiCT α and β priors} (\textbf{RUN9}), which define the shape and scale of the surplus production function and thus control the curvature and productivity of the production relationship. These α and β priors were considered as an alternative prior set but were ultimately \textbf{kept disabled} in the main analyses, as their inclusion was intended solely for diagnostic purposes rather than for defining the final model configurations. To generally stabilise estimation, SPiCT imposes default semi-informative priors on these quantities, preventing them from taking extreme and unrealistic values (\citeproc{ref-Pedersen2023SPiCTHandbook}{Pedersen, Kokkalis, Mildenberger, \& Berg, 2023}).

All scenarios were also tested under an alternative prior assumption for the intrinsic population growth rate, fixing the prior on \emph{r} at \textbf{0.5}, consistent with estimates reported in FishBase for \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} \href{https://www.sealifebase.ca/summary/Nephrops-norvegicus.html}{FishBase}. This additional configuration was used to evaluate the sensitivity of model outcomes to assumptions about population productivity.

Overall, this expanded hierarchical design enables a more comprehensive and systematic evaluation of prior influence across scenarios, while maintaining consistency and comparability among model configurations (Table (\citeproc{ref-ref}{\textbf{ref?}})(tab:spictpriors2)).

\subsection{Results}\label{results}

\subsubsection{Previous Analysis}\label{previous-analysis}

The exploratory modelling scenarios SC0 to SC5 implemented in the present analysis are documented in detail in the technical report by Vila \& Quinzán (\citeproc{ref-VilaQuinzan2024}{2024}), where alternative configurations of fishery-dependent and fishery-independent data were systematically evaluated using the SPiCT framework for \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} in Functional Unit 30. These scenarios form part of the broader exploratory analyses conducted to assess model behaviour, data sensitivity, and the robustness of stock status estimates under different information assumptions.

The following sections present a synthesis of the key findings from these new scenarios, including diagnostic evaluations, stock dynamics, and management implications, while also referencing relevant results from the previously documented scenarios (\texttt{SC0} to \texttt{SC4}) for comparative purposes.

\subsubsection{New Scenarios}\label{new-scenarios}

\paragraph{Correlation analysis of input data series}\label{correlation-analysis-of-input-data-series}

The Figure \ref{fig:correlation} reveals strong positive correlations among indices derived from similar sources, particularly between ISUNEPCA UWTV biomass and abundance indices, as well as between standardized CPUE and effort-related metrics. In contrast, ARSA-based indices show weaker or negative correlations with some fishery-dependent indicators, highlighting differences in spatial coverage and survey design. Overall, the results indicate a clear clustering of indices by data origin, suggesting partial redundancy among some series and complementarity across others.

\paragraph{Population Variables}\label{population-variables}

In the new explorations, in this report we focus on the results obtained from the additional scenarios (\texttt{SC5} to \texttt{SC8}) that incorporate alternative combinations of abundance indices corrected and fishery-dependent information.

To optimise the presentation and interpretation of model outputs for the SPiCT assessment of \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} in Functional Unit 30, only scenarios and prior configurations that successfully converged are retained for reporting. For these converged runs, we present the key summary tables and diagnostic and management relevant figures, ensuring that all results shown are based on numerically stable and internally consistent model solutions. This approach facilitates a clear and robust comparison of alternative data configurations and prior assumptions while avoiding misleading inferences from non-convergent model fits.

The estimated biomass trajectories for Nephrops FU30 across scenarios SC5 to SC9 are illustrated in Figures \ref{fig:plotspictsc5r5}--\ref{fig:plotspictsc9r8}.

\paragraph{Hindcast Analysis}\label{hindcast-analysis}

The Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) is a widely used metric for evaluating the predictive accuracy of time series models. It is defined as the mean absolute error of the model's predictions, scaled by the mean absolute error of a naive forecast (typically the previous observation). MASE values below 1 indicate that the model outperforms the naive forecast, while values above 1 suggest inferior performance.

Hindcast diagnostics by scenario and prior configuration are presented in Figures
\ref{fig:hindcastsc0run4} and \ref{fig:hindcastsc1run1}--\ref{fig:hindcastsc2run4}, illustrating model predictive performance under alternative data and prior combinations.

\paragraph{Retrospective Analysis}\label{retrospective-analysis}

Retrospective patterns for each modelling scenario were evaluated under alternative prior configurations.
Scenario-specific diagnostics are shown in Figures \ref{fig:retroSC0RUN4}--\ref{fig:retroSC5RUN4},
illustrating the temporal stability of biomass and fishing mortality estimates across successive peelings. Retrospective bias was evaluated using Mohn's rho estimates (Table \ref{tab:mohnsrho}). Treshold to consider a model as acceptable is set to (+-)0.20 in mohn 's rho values for this kind os species (\citeproc{ref-Hurtado-ferro2015}{Hurtado-ferro et al., 2015}).

The results indicate that all model runs achieved MASE values below 1, demonstrating that the SPiCT model consistently outperformed the naive forecast in predicting stock dynamics. Notably, RUN3 exhibited the lowest MASE value, suggesting that the inclusion of informative priors on both initial depletion and production curve shape enhanced model predictive accuracy. These findings underscore the importance of prior specification in improving model performance and provide confidence in the SPiCT model's ability to capture key stock dynamics for Nephrops FU30.

\subsubsection{Comparative Population variables}\label{comparative-population-variables}

Scenario-specific stock dynamics derived from the SPiCT model are illustrated through estimated biomass
(Figures \ref{figBiomassSC0}--\ref{figBiomassSC5}) and fishing mortality trajectories
(Figures \ref{figFishingMortalitySC0}--\ref{figFishingMortalitySC5}), allowing direct comparison of model behaviour across alternative data configurations.

\subsubsection{Biological References Ponits.}\label{biological-references-ponits.}

The Figure \ref{fig:brps} compares key biological reference points across \textbf{Scenarios SC5--SC9} and \textbf{prior configurations (RUN1--RUN4)} for Nephrops FU30. Several clear patterns emerge. Estimates of \textbf{Bmsy} and \textbf{MSY} vary widely across RUNs within the same scenario. RUN1--RUN3 typically yield \textbf{much higher Bmsy and MSY} than RUN4, indicating that the more informative priors in RUN4 (on depletion, productivity, and growth) strongly constrain stock productivity and carrying capacity. This implies that data alone are not sufficient to tightly identify absolute scale, and priors play a major role in determining long-term productivity. Across scenarios, \textbf{Fmsy} is far less variable than \textbf{Bmsy} or \textbf{MSY}, particularly under RUN4, where Fmsy consistently clusters around \textbf{\textasciitilde0.12--0.15}. This suggests that exploitation rate at MSY is relatively well informed by the time series, even when absolute biomass is uncertain.

Scenarios \textbf{SC7 and SC8}, which combine \textbf{normalized UWTV biomass, ARSA biomass, and/or standardized CPUE}, show tighter clustering across RUNs compared with SC5--SC6. Adding complementary fishery-independent and fishery-dependent indices improves identifiability, especially for Fmsy and Cp. SC9 displays extreme or unstable MSY and Bmsy for some RUNs, reflecting the mixed time coverage (UWTV 2015--2025, ARSA biomass 1993--2012, CPUE 2009--2024) and the survey break in 2012. This configuration increases structural uncertainty and makes results more prior-dependent.

Catch suggested is broadly similar across RUNs within scenarios, indicating that recent catches are well constrained by data and largely independent of prior assumptions. Main values in Table \ref{tab:bprstable}.

Management strategy evaluation results by scenario are summarised in Table \ref{tab:managementsummary}. Values correspond to equilibrium projections for relative biomass (B/Bmsy), fishing mortality (F/Fmsy), and expected catches under each management rule.

\subsection{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

(todo esto es preliminar, falta integrar resultados de las nuevas corridas)

WGBIE considered the results obtained for Nephrops FU 30 stock promising and will propose it as possible candidate for the next WKMSYSPiCT benchmark. However, further work should be conducted to improve the model according to the most recent SPiCT guidelines (\citeproc{ref-Mildenberger2023SPiCT}{Mildenberger, Kokkalis, \& Berg, 2023}). The WKMSYSPiCT benchmark, where a close contact with SPiCT expert is possible, could help to find the best model configuration to provide the assessment for this stock.
Further work is needed to improve the model, including:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Extend the time series of landings to earlier years in order to improve the stability and robustness of the model
\item
  Add uncertainty in some years of the biomass indices time series or catches
\item
  Fine-tune model configuration.
\end{itemize}

The results indicate that all model runs achieved MASE values below 1, demonstrating that the SPiCT model consistently outperformed the naive forecast in predicting stock dynamics. Notably, RUN3 exhibited the lowest MASE value, suggesting that the inclusion of informative priors on both initial depletion and production curve shape enhanced model predictive accuracy. These findings underscore the importance of prior specification in improving model performance and provide confidence in the SPiCT model's ability to capture key stock dynamics for Nephrops FU30.

\textbf{Implication for management and benchmarking}
RUN4 provides the most conservative and stable estimates of productivity and fishing mortality across scenarios. SC7--SC8 appear to offer the best balance between information content and robustness, whereas SC9 should be treated cautiously due to survey discontinuity--driven uncertainty.
