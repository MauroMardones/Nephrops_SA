\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

The Surplus Production in Continuous Time (SPiCT) model has been proposed by ICES to provide MSY-based advice for a number of Category 3 stocks during the benchmark workshop WKBMSYSPiCT-4. \emph{Nephrops} in FU 30 is one of these stocks. Available data and some exploratory runs were presented at the Data Meeting held in December 2025 to evaluate data suitability. Experts recommended the use of the ISUNEPCA UWTV survey, as this independent fishery index is considered the most reliable \emph{Nephrops} abundance index and is currently used as the base of the assessment and advice. However, the time series is relatively short for application in the SPiCT model and an additional biomass index is required. The independent fishery ARSA IBTS survey index and the commercial LPUE index are other available data sources. Nevertheless, both indices should be standardized before their use in the assessment. Regarding to set the priors in the model, the use of initial biomass depletion prior (B/K) should be fully justified. The purpose of the present working document is to present the new SPiCT model explorations for \emph{Nephrops} in FU 30 taking these recommendations into account.

\subsection{Introduction}\label{introduction}

The Nephrops stock in FU 30 (Division 9.a) (Figure \ref{fig:areaconcept}) is considered a Category 3 stock. Despite the availability of a UWTV survey abundance index in FU 30, the advice follows a data-limited stock approach because an FMSY proxy has not been defined for this stock, as required for Category 1 Nephrops stocks (\citeproc{ref-ICES2015}{Acom, 2015}). Last advice given in 2025 followed the \emph{`rfb'} rule according to the recent ICES guidelines (\citeproc{ref-ICES2015}{Acom, 2015}). This has marked a transition from the precautionary approach based on the 2-over-3 rule, applied from 2019 to 2024 advice, to the MSY approach using the ICES \emph{`rfb'} rule. Nevertheless, the current advice remained precautionary and is now provided on a biennial basis.

The stochastic surplus production model in continuous time (SPiCT) has been recommended by ICES for a number of data-limited stocks to provide MSY-based advice. SPiCT has been applied in the assessment of several Nephrops stocks, including three in Iberian waters (FU 25, FU 26--27, and FU 31) (\citeproc{ref-ICES2015}{Acom, 2015}; \citeproc{ref-GonzalezHerraiz2023}{González Herraiz et al., 2023}) and one in the northern North Sea (FU 32) (\citeproc{ref-ICES2015}{Acom, 2015}; \citeproc{ref-ICES2023a}{ICES, 2023b}). Some SPiCT explorations were conducted during the WGBIE 2024 to evaluate the potential of this model for the assessment of Nephrops in FU 30 and to propose this stock as a possible candidate for the WKMSYSPiCT-4 benchmark (\citeproc{ref-VilaQuinzan2024}{Vila \& Quinzán, 2024}). The results were also presented at the last Data Meeting held in December 2025. Based on the exploratory runs and expert discussions, several recommendations were made regarding data inputs and prior configurations for the SPiCT model. The ISUNEPCA UWTV survey index was identified as the most reliable abundance index for Nephrops in FU 30 and is currently used as the basis for assessment and advice (\citeproc{ref-ICES2023b}{ICES, 2023a}). However, given the relatively short time series of this survey, it was recommended to incorporate an additional biomass index to enhance model robustness.

This working document extends the exploratory framework by incorporating new SPiCT scenarios developed following discussions after the December meeting. Specifically, a new set of scenarios is introduced based on the recently standardized indices time series, all configured consistently with previous analyses by applying alternative prior configurations to each scenario.

\subsection{Methods}\label{methods}

\subsubsection{SPiCT description}\label{spict-description}

Surplus productions models are widely used in fisheries stock assessment, particularly for data-limited stocks. These models describe the dynamics of the total biomass of a fish stock over time, considering the balance between growth (production) and removals (catches). The basic equation governing surplus production models is:
\[
\frac{dB(t)}{dt} = P(B(t)) - C(t)
\]
where:
- \(B(t)\) is the biomass at time \(t\),
- \(P(B(t))\) is the surplus production function, representing the net growth of the biomass,
- \(C(t)\) is the catch at time \(t\).

In a graphical inspection, a surplus production curve typically has a dome-shaped form, indicating that production increases with biomass up to a certain point (the maximum sustainable yield, MSY), after which it declines due to density-dependent effects. The shape of curve dependes on the specific functional form chosen for \(P(B)\), such as the Schaefer model, Pella-Tomlinson model, or others. (Figure \ref{fig:surplusplot}).

The SPiCT model extends traditional surplus production models by incorporating stochastic elements and continuous-time dynamics. This allows for a more realistic representation of the uncertainties inherent in fish stock assessments, such as environmental variability and observation errors. The key features of the SPiCT model include:
- \textbf{Stochasticity}: The model incorporates process and observation errors, allowing for the quantification of uncertainty in biomass estimates and reference points.
- \textbf{Continuous-time framework}: Unlike discrete-time models, SPiCT operates in continuous time, providing a more flexible and accurate representation of stock dynamics.
- \textbf{Multiple data sources}: SPiCT can integrate various types of data, including catch data, abundance indices from surveys, and fishery-dependent indices such as CPUE and fishing effort.
- \textbf{Bayesian inference}: The model employs Bayesian methods for parameter estimation, enabling the incorporation of prior knowledge and the derivation of posterior distributions for model parameters and stock status indicators.

\subsubsection{SPICT Explorations}\label{spict-explorations}

The analyses were made with R version 4.5.2. and the package SPiCT (\citeproc{ref-Pedersen2017}{Pedersen \& Berg, 2017}) (version 1.3.8.1 recommended), following the Handbook and Guidelines developed for this package (\citeproc{ref-Mildenberger2023SPiCT}{Mildenberger, Kokkalis, \& Berg, 2023}; \citeproc{ref-Pedersen2023SPiCTHandbook}{Pedersen, Kokkalis, Mildenberger, \& Berg, 2023}).

\subsubsection{Available Data Sources}\label{available-data-sources}

The summary of the input data series used for SPiCT explorations for \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} in Functional Unit 30 is presented in Table \ref{tab:inputdata}. The data sources include:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Total catches} (1987--2024) (Figure \ref{fig:landings}).
\item
  \textbf{Fishing effort} (1994--2024), expressed as total fishing days.
\item
  \textbf{Standardised landings per unit of effort (CPUE)} (2009--2025).
\item
  \textbf{ISUNEPCA UWTV survey indices}, including:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    absolute \textbf{abundance index} (available from 2015--2025), and
  \item
    associated \textbf{biomass index} (available for selected years).
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{ARSA survey indices}, comprising:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    \textbf{spring survey index} (1993--2025) (Figure \ref{fig:indices}).
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

Annual Nephrops landings in FU30 show strong variability over the analyzed period with increasing landings during the late 1990s and early 2000s, followed by a progressive decline mainly associated with reductions in fishing effort due to the implementation of various Spanish fishing management plans. The special limitations of TAC imposed by EU during the 2013-2015 period due the sanction applied in 2012 for exceeding the TAC, led to a further drop in landings. Since 2016, landings trend is declining and remain at lower levels, reflecting both stock availability and TAC constraints.

Fishery-independent information is provided by the ISUNEPCA UWTV survey, conducted annually since 2015 (except 2020), which delivers absolute abundance estimates in accordance with ICES standards (\citeproc{ref-Dobby2019nepref}{Dobby, 2019}). The ISUNEPCA biomass index can be derived either multiplying by the annual mean weight in landings or using the average mean weight of the three previous years. The ARSA IBTS survey provides a relative biomass index for Nephrops in deeper strata of FU30, where this species is distributed. Fishing effort and commercial LPUE estimated using trips in which Nephrops landings (by weight) represented at least 10\% of the total catch are available. However, experts recommended not using these data series, as they were not considered appropriate for use as inputs in the SPiCT model. In addition, a standardized commercial LPUE series, derived from the unique trawl métier operating in FU 30, provides a more accurate fishery-dependent index of relative biomass.

\subsubsection{Scenarios and model run configurations}\label{scenarios-and-model-run-configurations}

\quad

During the Data Meeting held in December, several combinations of available data sources were defined and evaluated to explore alternative model configurations within the SPiCT framework. These preliminary analyses comprised scenarios \textbf{SC0 to SC4}, together with prior sets \textbf{RUN1 to RUN4}, and formed the basis for the initial assessment work (Table \ref{tab:scenarios} and Table \ref{tab:spictpriors}).

Subsequently, for the Assessment Meeting, new scenarios incorporating alternative indices available after December were tested. The present document therefore focuses on scenarios \textbf{SC5 to SC9}, which extend the original framework by exploring updated data combinations, as well as an expanded range of prior configurations \textbf{RUN5 to RUN12} designed to further evaluate model sensitivity and robustness. All scenarios were also tested under an alternative prior assumption for the intrinsic population growth rate, fixing the prior on \emph{r} at \textbf{0.5}, \textbf{0.4}, consistent with estimates reported in FishBase for \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} \href{https://www.sealifebase.ca/summary/Nephrops-norvegicus.html}{FishBase}. This additional configuration was used to evaluate the sensitivity of model outcomes to assumptions about population productivity. All scenarios considered are summarized in Table \ref{tab:newscenario}, while Table \ref{tab:spictpriors2} provides an overview of the corresponding prior configurations applied to each scenario.

The prior configuration used in this new set of priors, defines both the biological structure and the stochastic components of the SPICT state-space production model. Priors on \texttt{logalpha}, \texttt{logbeta}, and \texttt{logn} activate a flexible Pella--Tomlinson formulation, allowing deviations from the classical Schaefer model by relaxing assumptions on the shape and symmetry of the production curve. The prior on \texttt{logr} constrains the intrinsic population growth rate, providing biological anchoring that improves model stability. Process variability in biomass dynamics is controlled through \texttt{logsdb}, while \texttt{logsdc} explicitly allows for substantial uncertainty in reported catches, reducing the likelihood of conflict between observed removals and modeled population dynamics. Variability in fishing mortality is restricted through \texttt{logsdf}, promoting smoother exploitation trajectories over time. Finally, the prior on \texttt{logbkfrac} governs the initial depletion level of the stock relative to carrying capacity, influencing early biomass trajectories and helping to regularize model initialization when early data are limited or uninformative.

The spin-up option was enabled in all scenarios (\texttt{inp\$nspinup\ \textless{}-\ 16}), allowing the model to start prior to the first available data point. This approach, following the recommendations, facilitates model stabilization and reduces sensitivity to initial conditions. In addition, for all scenarios, the initial coefficient of variation (CV) and initial state conditions were specified consistently with the observational framework described, ensuring a coherent treatment of early dynamics across model configurations.

\subsection{Results}\label{results}

\subsubsection{Overview of scenarios and model performance}\label{overview-of-scenarios-and-model-performance}

This report focuses on the results obtained from the additional scenarios (\textbf{SC5--SC9}), which explore alternative combinations of corrected abundance indices and fishery-dependent information within the SPiCT framework for \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} in Functional Unit 30. To ensure robustness and interpretability, only scenarios and prior configurations that achieved numerical convergence and passed basic diagnostic checks are retained for detailed presentation (Table \ref{tab:retrosummary}). Summary tables and diagnostic figures are therefore based exclusively on stable and internally consistent model solutions, allowing meaningful comparison across alternative data configurations and prior assumptions while avoiding inference from non-convergent runs.

Estimated biomass trajectories across scenarios \textbf{SC5--SC9} are presented in Figures 7--8.

\subsubsection{Convergence and diagnostic patterns}\label{convergence-and-diagnostic-patterns}

The initial convergence screening across scenarios and prior configurations reveals a clear and consistent pattern. Scenarios that do \textbf{not} include the ARSA yield-based index---most notably \textbf{SC5} and \textbf{SC6}, which rely on ISUNEPCA biomass and abundance indices---tend to converge more reliably and show fewer diagnostic issues across the majority of prior sets. In contrast, scenarios that incorporate the ARSA series (\textbf{SC7} and \textbf{SC9}) exhibit a substantially higher frequency of convergence failures and diagnostic inconsistencies.

These issues are most likely driven by the limited informativeness of the ARSA series, combined with its temporal discontinuity and mismatch with other data sources, which complicates parameter estimation and increases sensitivity to prior assumptions.

For the purposes of this report, diagnostic results are therefore presented comparatively for \textbf{SC6} and \textbf{SC9}, representing, respectively, a scenario with relatively strong overall performance and one with clearly suboptimal behaviour. Nevertheless, all scenarios and model outputs have been retained and remain available for transparency and further investigation.

\subsubsection{Data relationships}\label{data-relationships}

Correlation analysis among input data series (Figure \ref{fig:correlation}) shows strong positive correlations between indices derived from similar sources, particularly between ISUNEPCA UWTV biomass and abundance indices, as well as among standardized CPUE and effort-related metrics. In contrast, ARSA-based indices display weaker and sometimes negative correlations with several fishery-dependent indicators. This pattern reflects differences in survey design, spatial coverage, and temporal consistency. Overall, indices cluster clearly by data origin, indicating partial redundancy within data groups and limited complementarity between ARSA and other sources.

\subsubsection{Population dynamics}\label{population-dynamics}

Population dynamics inferred from converged model runs indicate broadly consistent biomass and fishing mortality trajectories across scenarios dominated by ISUNEPCA indices and standardized CPUE. Biomass trajectories for scenarios \textbf{SC5--SC9} are shown in Figures \ref{fig:plotspictsc5r5}--\ref{fig:plotspictsc9r8}. Scenarios including ARSA tend to show higher variability and less stable trajectories, particularly under less informative prior configurations, reinforcing concerns regarding the influence of this index on model behaviour.

\subsubsection{Predictive performance}\label{predictive-performance}

Hindcast performance was evaluated using the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE). All converged model runs yielded MASE values below 1, indicating that SPiCT consistently outperformed a naive benchmark forecast. Differences among prior configurations were modest, although runs including informative priors on initial depletion and production curve shape generally exhibited slightly improved predictive accuracy. These results support the suitability of SPiCT for capturing key stock dynamics in FU30 when supported by coherent data inputs.

\subsubsection{Retrospective consistency}\label{retrospective-consistency}

Retrospective analyses were conducted for all converged scenarios under alternative prior configurations. Results indicate that scenarios dominated by ISUNEPCA indices and standardized CPUE display relatively stable retrospective patterns, with limited systematic bias in biomass and fishing mortality estimates. In contrast, scenarios incorporating ARSA frequently failed retrospective diagnostics due to non-convergence. Mohn's rho estimates for acceptable runs generally fall within commonly applied thresholds for short-lived species, although performance deteriorates markedly in ARSA-based configurations.

\subsubsection{Reference points}\label{reference-points}

Estimates of biological reference points vary substantially across prior configurations, particularly for \textbf{B\_MSY} and \textbf{MSY}, highlighting the strong influence of priors on absolute biomass scale. In contrast, \textbf{F\_MSY} shows considerably lower variability across scenarios, suggesting that exploitation rates are more strongly informed by the data. Scenarios combining ISUNEPCA indices with standardized CPUE exhibit more coherent and interpretable reference point estimates, whereas \textbf{SC9} shows extreme or unstable values under several prior sets, reflecting structural uncertainty associated with mixed and discontinuous data coverage.

\subsection{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

The results presented in this report confirm that SPiCT provides a viable and informative assessment framework for \emph{Nephrops norvegicus} in Functional Unit 30 when supported by coherent and informative data inputs. However, model performance is strongly scenario-dependent. Across nearly all prior configurations, scenarios incorporating the ARSA yield-based index show persistent convergence and diagnostic problems, indicating that this series is poorly informative for the assessment and difficult for the model to reconcile with other data sources.

In contrast, scenarios based on \textbf{ISUNEPCA survey indices}, particularly when combined with the \textbf{new standardized CPUE}, consistently demonstrate better convergence, more stable diagnostics, and more coherent population trajectories. These configurations therefore represent the most robust basis for further assessment development.

From a management and benchmarking perspective, scenarios dominated by ISUNEPCA indices and standardized CPUE provide the best balance between information content and model stability, while scenarios relying heavily on ARSA should be treated with caution. Further work should focus on refining these better-performing configurations in line with recent SPiCT guidelines, with particular attention to data uncertainty, model simplification, and diagnostic performance.
